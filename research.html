Traffic Routing and Congestion Optimisation

This project simulates congestion-aware routing on Oxford's real road network to investigate how traffic concentrates and whether re-routing and learned congestion prediction can spread it out.

Methods included graph modelling with OSMnx and NetworkX, Dijkstra's algorithm and A*, dynamic edge weighting, and a feed-forward neural network implemented from scratch in NumPy.

Read the report (PDF) · Source code

Key Findings

  • A* gave no speed-up over Dijkstra: its straight-line heuristic was measured in degrees while road costs were in seconds, so it gave almost no guidance.
  • Congestion concentrated on a few corridors: the busiest road segment carried 11–13% of all journeys.
  • One round of re-routing changed most vehicles' routes and moved bottlenecks to other corridors rather than removing them.
  • The neural network did not train (test R² = −0.09); the report traces this to its learning rate and weight initialisation and sets out the fix.
